## 2. Key findings

### Finding 1: Revenue grew 25% year over year
2023 revenue was $7.11M and 2024 was $8.89M (+25.1%). Every quarter grew, and growth accelerated through the year
(Q1 +20.5% → Q4 +30.6%).
*Source: `sql/03_comparative_analysis.sql` (3.1), notebook section 6.*

### Finding 2: Strong Q4 seasonality
November and December are the two biggest months in both years. In 2024, December hit $1.05M, 
about 50% higher than the typical month. Nov and Dec together make up about 22% of annual revenue.
*Source: `sql/02_trend_analysis.sql` (2.1, 2.2), Power BI page 2.*
**Action:** plan inventory and campaigns ahead of October.

### Finding 3: Electronics drives the business
Electronics brings in 53% of revenue ($8.5M) but has the lowest margin (27.4%). Clothing has the highest margin (32.4%) 
but only 10% of revenue.
*Source: `sql/01_business_metrics.sql` (1.2), Power BI page 3.*
**Action:** protect Electronics volume, and test growing Clothing since it is more profitable per dollar.

### Finding 4: Discounts cut margin sharply
40% of orders carry a discount. Margin falls from 31.7% (no discount) to 13.7% at a 20% discount.
A t-test confirms discounted orders earn less profit ($153 vs $220 per order, p < 0.001).
*Source: `sql/04_aggregations.sql` (4.2), notebook section 2.1.*
**Action:** limit 20% discounts, and check whether they actually increase quantity sold.

### Finding 5: West region lags behind
West revenue is $3.29M, about 26% below North ($4.45M), with the fewest orders (4,945).
North, South and East are close together. Average order value is the same in all regions (ANOVA p = 0.60),
so the gap comes from **fewer orders**, not smaller orders.
*Source: `sql/03_comparative_analysis.sql` (3.2), notebook section 2.2.*
**Action:** focus on acquiring or reactivating customers in the West.

### Finding 6: Customer base is not very concentrated
The top 20% of customers generate 27.6% of revenue (far from the 80/20 rule). Consumer is the largest segment (48%),
but average order value is similar across segments (~$656–$665).
Revenue depends on many mid-sized customers, so there is low risk from losing any single account.
*Source: notebook section 4, Power BI page 4.*

### Finding 7: Weekday vs weekend makes no difference
Revenue per day (about $21.8K vs $22.2K) and order value (p = 0.80) are basically the same.
No need for weekend-specific promotions.
*Source: `sql/03_comparative_analysis.sql` (3.4), notebook section 2.3.*

## 3. Recommendations (summary)

1. Prepare for Q4: stock and marketing from October.
2. Tighten discount rules, especially the 20% tier.
3. Build a West-region growth plan.
4. Test Clothing growth for better margin mix.
5. Keep tracking MoM and YoY growth in the Power BI dashboard.

## 4. Limitations
- Synthetic data, no returns/refunds, no customer join dates (so no true retention or churn analysis).
- Only 2 years of data, so seasonality is based on two cycles.
- Profit uses unit cost only (no shipping or marketing cost).
